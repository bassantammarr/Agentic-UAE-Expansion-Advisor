# Operations, Supply Chain and Staffing

## Supply chain
- The UAE imports most of its food; Al Aweer and Abu Dhabi central markets and large distributors supply fresh produce.
- Halal meat suppliers are widely available (local, Australian, Indian, Brazilian, Pakistani sources).
- Signature Jordanian ingredients (spice blends, specific cheeses, breads) may need import or local recreation; secure two suppliers for critical items.
- Lock in meat pricing with quarterly contracts to manage cost volatility.

## Central kitchen
- A central prep kitchen keeps recipes consistent across branches and lowers labour per site.
- Prepare marinated meats, doughs and sauces centrally; finish in branches.

## Staffing
- Most kitchen and service staff are hired on employer-sponsored visas; budget for visa, medical, Emirates ID, housing or allowance and annual flight tickets.
- Hire a Jordanian head chef to protect authenticity, supported by trained local teams.
- Arabic and English speaking front-of-house staff improve service for both Arab and expat customers.
- Plan for staff turnover; document recipes and SOPs from day one.

## Operating hours
- Late-night demand is strong in Dubai; consider opening until 1–2 AM in residential and nightlife areas.
- Adjust hours during Ramadan.

## Key expansion risks
- High rent relative to revenue in prime locations.
- Aggregator commission eroding delivery margins.
- Intense competition from established shawarma and pizza chains.
- Summer seasonality reducing dine-in traffic.
- Ingredient availability for authentic recipes.
- Licensing delays pushing back opening dates.
